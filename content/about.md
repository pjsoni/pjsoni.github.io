## My Journey into Technology

I've been fascinated by computers for as long as I can remember, especially the machines that powered early video games. My first hands-on experience with a computer came when I had the chance to play games on a **Spectravideo SVI-728** at a friend's house. That experience sparked my curiosity about personal computers and started a journey that eventually became a career in technology.

My first introduction to programming came through **BASIC**. Writing simple programs and seeing an idea turn into something that actually worked was fascinating to me. What started as childhood curiosity eventually grew into more than **20 years of experience in software development**.

Early in my career, I worked with **Classic ASP**, which gave me a foundation in web development and eventually led me into the Microsoft technology ecosystem. Over the years, my focus evolved through **VB.NET, C#, ASP.NET, .NET, and .NET Core**, along with the technologies needed to build complete applications and services.

I've worked on a wide variety of systems, from web applications and APIs to enterprise integrations and distributed services. Some of that work has included software supporting airline booking, seat selection, partner integrations, and operational systems. Along the way, I've also taken on responsibilities as a technical lead and architect, helping design solutions and solve problems that go beyond simply writing code.

## What Drives Me

What keeps me interested in technology is the combination of **building things, solving problems, and learning something new**.

I enjoy working through difficult technical problems, whether that means designing an application, tracking down a performance issue, improving a database query, or figuring out how different systems can work together. I'm particularly interested in the space where software development, infrastructure, and DevOps meet.

I'm also a strong fan of **open-source software and technologies**. I don't just follow open-source projects; I actively use them, experiment with them, and incorporate them into solutions when they make sense. I appreciate being able to understand how something works, customize it when necessary, contribute back when I can, and learn from the work of others.

That interest extends well beyond my professional work. My homelab has become something of a personal technology playground where I experiment with **self-hosting, virtualization, containers, networking, storage, automation, observability, and local AI**. It gives me an opportunity to explore technologies in ways that aren't always possible in a production environment and, just as importantly, to break things and figure out how to fix them.

Many of the projects and technologies I explore there eventually influence how I approach problems at work. Likewise, ideas and lessons from professional software development often find their way into my personal projects.

## Why I Share What I Learn

This website is an extension of that curiosity.

I use it to document things I'm learning, projects I'm working on, problems I've solved, and technologies I'm exploring. Some articles are carefully worked-out tutorials; others are simply notes about something that took me longer to figure out than it probably should have.

I believe one of the best ways to understand something is to explain it to someone else. Writing also gives me a reason to go back, organize what I've learned, and turn a solution that worked once into something that can be useful again.

The goal is simple: **learn something, build something, and share what I learned.**

## Beyond Technology

Technology is a big part of what I do, but it isn't all I enjoy.

I'm a fan of **science fiction, music, photography, and creative pursuits**. I'm also an enthusiast of the Microsoft ecosystem, while at the same time enjoying the diversity of tools and ideas that come from the broader open-source community.

What continues to fascinate me is how all of these technologies evolve over time. The tools I use today would have seemed remarkably different when I first started programming, and I enjoy being part of that ongoing evolution.
